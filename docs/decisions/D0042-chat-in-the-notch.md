# D0042: The chat is the notch grown large, one click away, not a window of its own

Date: 2026-11-24. Status: active. No foreman task (asked for in a session by the owner). Adds a fourth state to the dock of [D0035](D0035-dock-from-the-notch.md), [D0038](D0038-dock-session-switch.md) and [D0040](D0040-dock-switch-and-command-keys.md), and changes where the chat view opens from the shell ([D0003](D0003-windows-shell-choices.md), [D0010](D0010-dock-fixed-window.md)).

## Context
The owner, on 2026-11-24: "make it so that its only one click to go from notch to chat, and so that the chat is just a larger notch you know, super smooth transition and everything, not a separate window". Until now the dock's "…" → "Open in chat", the tray's "Open chat" and every link to `/` opened the chat view in a window of its own: a second, ordinary window with its own frame and backdrop, a few hundred pixels from the notch it was asked for from.

## Decision
- **A fourth state, `chat`.** The dock's states are idle, peek, open and chat. In chat the island is the whole chat view, hanging from the notch (or its stand-in, or the top edge off a Mac) in the same black, or the same dark glass without a notch. The shell takes the mouse on the chat's whole rectangle and lets the window take the keyboard, as in open.
- **One click.** A click on the pill or a peek grows the island into the chat straight away, on the session the dock would open on (the one peeking first). On the open dock a click on the notch's band above the head, or on the head where there is no control, does the same, on the session shown; so does the head's expand button (two arrows outwards, at its right end) and "Open in chat" in its "…". Hover still opens the small dock after 80 ms: the click is the second gesture, and comes naturally after the hover, at the very top where the pointer already is. Which click does what is one pure function, `clickAction` in `dock-state.js`.
- **Back down, a step at a time.** Esc in the chat (that nothing in the chat view took first: its search, a menu, a rename and its dialog keep theirs), the chat's own back-to-the-dock button in its sidebar head, or a click on the notch's band steps down to the open dock on the session the chat showed; Esc there closes it. Stepped down, the open dock stays until the pointer has been on it and left, since the pointer is wherever the chat was. A press beside the chat, or another app taking the keyboard, closes it to idle at once. The pointer leaving the chat does not: it is a place to read and type, not a hover card.
- **The window is as large as the largest state from the start.** The shell works out the chat's size for the primary display (`chatSize` in `shell/src/dock.ts`): 62% of the area's width and 76% of its height, at most 1180x860, never less than the open dock (460x480), at least 24 px clear of the sides and the bottom, in even pixels. A 13-inch MacBook Air (1470x956) gets 910x726, a 1920x1080 screen 1180x820. The window is that size (`dockWindow`) for the dock's whole life and is never resized or moved for a state, as before ([D0010](D0010-dock-fixed-window.md)); outside the island's rectangle it lets the mouse through, as before. The page gets the size as `/dock?notch=WxH&chat=WxH` (`dockUrl`, `parseChat`), and a change of display that changes it loads the page again, as a new notch does. From the display's bounds, not its work area, so a Dock or menu bar that hides does not change it.
- **The content is the chat view itself.** The island's chat layer holds one frame on `/?embed=dock`, the same page as `/`, loaded once with the dock and kept laid out at its full size while out of sight (`visibility: hidden` and `inert`, never `display: none`), so growing draws what is already there and nothing reflows on the way. In embed mode the chat view is dark, with no backdrop and no glass, two columns of the island's own colour; its links to other pages (New task, Settings) open as windows of the shell; it takes the keyboard only while the dock shows it; its history is left alone; ⌘K finds a session. The dock and the frame talk through `window.holmEmbed` (`show(id)`, `hide()`, `selected()`, the dock calling into the frame on its own origin) and one message back, `{ holmEmbed: "down" }`, taken only from that frame on the runner's origin. The chat view's code is not copied: the whole of it, sessions grouped and searched, the whole conversation, tool cards, approvals and questions, the reply box, Rename, Pin, Archive, Fork and Delete, is the chat view's.
- **The motion.** Growing into the chat, the height leads on `--ease-soft` and the width follows `--lag` twice later: down first, then out. Stepping down is the reverse on the same spring, the width first. The island is never drawn larger than its state's rectangle: `--cap-w` and `--cap-h` hold the clip in open and the chat, so a spring's overshoot flattens there as it used to against the window's edge, and coming down from the chat the open dock's cap waits until the shape is inside it (`data-from="chat"`). The chat layer pops in from `--pop` at the top with a fade, a moment behind the shape, and goes with a fade and a slight shrink; no blur on a surface that large.
- **The separate chat window is what is left when there is no dock.** The tray's "Open chat" and double-click, a link to `/?session=<id>` from any of the shell's pages, and the dock's own links grow the dock into the chat through a new one-way message from the shell (`intuition-dock:chat`, carrying a session id or nothing; the preload's `onChat`). Only with the dock off in the settings, or its page not loaded (the runner down, an old runner), do they open the chat window as before. `/` in a normal browser is unchanged.

## Why
- **One window, one shape.** A transition between two windows can't be smooth: one fades in where the other was, at the OS's pace, and on a Mac any window ordered in fades for about 230 ms (D0037). One clip on one window grows from the notch as the other states already do, and the shell does nothing for it but take the mouse and the keyboard.
- **A window sized for the largest state** over resizing it on the way: a window resized mid-animation draws a frame at the old size or the new one with the old content, and on a Mac a resized transparent window has flashed before. The cost is a larger transparent window that lets clicks through, which the dock already was.
- **The chat view in a frame** over a second chat built into the dock, or the chat view's code refactored into a module both pages mount: the chat view is about 1,150 lines of state over its own element ids, menus, a dialog and history handling. A frame gives the chat in the dock every one of its features now and every later change to it for free, on the same origin and the same runner, with one more WebSocket. The cost is a narrow bridge (show, hide, selected, and one message back), and a frame's colour scheme must match its page's, or Chromium paints it opaque.
- **The soft spring for the chat** over the bouncy one the other states use: a panel of 900 by 700 pixels bouncing reads as a toy, and overshoots past any reasonable margin. The soft spring settles with about 1% of overshoot, which the cap flattens.
- **Clicks, not hover, for the chat.** Hover is how the small dock opens and must stay cheap; growing a large panel under a pointer that only passed by would cover the person's work.

## Alternatives
- Resize the dock's window to the chat's size as it grows, or swap to a second window at the end of the animation: rejected above.
- A chat built from `dock.js`'s own stream with a sidebar added: it would duplicate the chat view's tool cards, history, menus and dialog, and drift from it.
- Keep the chat window and animate it out of the notch: two windows, two fades, and the chat window's frame and backdrop would never read as the notch.
- A double click on the notch: slower than one, and the first click would already have meant something.

## Risk
- The window is larger: on Windows and Linux a transparent window of about 1180x820 at the top centre. It takes the mouse only on the island; an opaque dock (Linux without a compositor) is resized per state as before, now to the chat's size too.
- Two WebSockets per dock instead of one, and the chat view's page loaded while nobody looks at it.
- The chat's size is fixed when the page loads: a Dock that appears later is not measured (the size is from the display's bounds, with room left for one).
- A focus check decides "another app took the keyboard" (`document.hasFocus()` 60 ms after a blur, on the page and on the frame): a window manager that blurs a window for its own reasons would close the chat.
