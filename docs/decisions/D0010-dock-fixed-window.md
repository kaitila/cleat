# D0010: The dock's window never changes size; the mouse goes through it outside the island

Date: 2026-11-13. Status: active. Replaces three points of [D0008](D0008-dock.md): the window's sizes, "the window resizes, it is not a click-through overlay", and the order of `setState` and the animation. The rest of D0008 stands (a dock instead of toasts, the one-word bridge, pushed notifications, the peek queue, what holds the dock open, dark in both schemes).

## What changed
- **One window size.** The dock's window is 460x480 CSS pixels for its whole life, at the top centre of the primary display. The shell sets its bounds when it is created and when the displays change, and never for a change of state: `dockBounds` in `shell/src/dock.ts` takes no state any more.
- **The island is drawn inside it.** The page's island element is as large as the window and never changes size either. What is seen of it is its `clip-path`, a rectangle hanging from the top centre. Each state has a rectangle the island never leaves: idle 128x22, peek 380x40, open 460x480 (`DOCK_SIZES` and `islandRect`, the same numbers in `shell/src/dock.ts` and `src/web/dock-state.js`; a test fails when they differ). Idle is drawn as narrow as its words, and 16 tall when nothing is going on; open is drawn as short as its content.
- **The mouse goes through the window, except on the island.** The main process decides, not the page. It looks at the cursor (`screen.getCursorScreenPoint`) every 20 ms while it is within 160 pixels of the island and every 120 ms otherwise, compares it with the rectangle of the state the page last reported through `setState`, and calls `setIgnoreMouseEvents` when the answer changes. The hit test is a pure function, `takesMouse`.
- **Click-through is the default.** The window is told to ignore the mouse the moment it is created. The shell holds no state for the page until the page reports one, and forgets it when the page starts a navigation or a reload, fails to load, crashes or is hidden. With no state, `takesMouse` is false wherever the cursor is.
- **`setState` is told at once**, growing and shrinking alike. `resizeOrder` is gone: there is no window resize to order the animation around.
- **The look.** Idle was 280x40 and is at most 128x22: a dot alone, 16 tall and faint, when nothing is going on; a few muted words when something runs; colour only for "needs you" and "failed". Peek was 420x76 with two lines and is 380x40 with one. Open was a fixed 460x600 and is as tall as its content, at most 480.
- **The motion.** One shape grows out of the pill: the clip animates over 320 ms on a curve that leaves fast and settles long without passing its size (`--settle`), and closes in 220 ms. The content of the new state fades in, four pixels down, once the shape is about 40% through its time, which on that curve is most of the way there in size; the old content fades out in 75 ms. Only `clip-path`, `opacity` and `transform` are animated. With `prefers-reduced-motion` the shape changes at once and the content fades in.
- **Opening** takes the pointer resting on the island for 80 ms (it was 120).

## Why
- **The owner saw it and said so.** On a real desktop on 2026-11-13: "make the notch more subtle and less tall. the animation is super janky and laggy, make it smoother, nicer, and all that jazz."
- **Resizing was the jank.** Windows resizes a transparent, frameless window slowly, and not in step with the page animating inside it: the page's frames and the window's sizes arrive at different times, so the island tears and jumps. No ordering of `setState` and the animation fixes that. A window that never changes leaves the animation to the compositor alone.
- **D0008 refused an overlay for fear of a dead zone**: a clear part of the screen that swallows clicks after a missed mouse-leave or a crash. That fear is answered here another way, by where the decision is made and what its default is:
  - The page is not asked where the mouse is. The main process reads the cursor itself, so a page that hangs, crashes or misses an event cannot leave the window taking clicks.
  - The window takes the mouse only inside a rectangle that is a pure function of one of three words. The worst a page can do is report "open" while it draws nothing: then 460x480 at the top centre takes clicks until the page reports otherwise, reloads or dies. That is the same worst case as before (the old window stuck open at 460x600), except that it is now invisible, which is why every way a page can go away resets the state to none.
  - A timer, not mouse events: a window that ignores the mouse gets no reliable enter and leave, and a missed leave was exactly the bug D0008 feared. Looking again every 20 ms cannot miss one for longer than that.
- **20 ms near, 120 ms far.** Near the island a late answer is felt: the first click on the pill would go to the window below. Far from it the only cost of looking is the timer, and the cursor cannot cross 160 pixels and click in much less than 120 ms.
- **Mouse moves are forwarded while ignoring** (`{ forward: true }`), so the page sees the pointer leave and closes on its own timer, as in a browser.
- **`clip-path`, not a size.** Animating the island's width and height lays out the session list and the stream on every frame. A clip changes no box: the content keeps its full size and place, and nothing reflows on the way. The edge is the island's own colour showing around a surface clipped one hairline smaller, since a border on a box that never changes size would be in the wrong place.
- **No bounce.** The island hangs from the screen's edge; a spring that overshoots would pull away from it or run past the window. The curve only approaches its size.
- **Faint when idle.** The dock sits over title bars and browser tabs all day. With nothing to say it should be findable and no more.

## What this costs
- **Under a short open panel the window still takes the mouse.** The open rectangle is the whole window, because the bridge carries one word and not a height. Where the panel is shorter than 480, the clear strip below it is the page's: a press there closes the dock at once (and is not passed on to the window below), and the pointer resting there closes it after 400 ms as anywhere else off the island.
- **The first few milliseconds on the pill.** A click that lands within 20 ms of the cursor arriving on the island can still go to the window below.
- **A timer runs for as long as the shell does**, about eight looks a second while the cursor is elsewhere.

## Not done here
- Nobody but the owner can see the real desktop. The browser script (`test/web/dock-ui.ts`) measures the island, times every frame of an open and a close in headless Chromium, and saves strips of frames; the shell's self-test reads the window's bounds in the three states and what the shell told the window about the mouse, with a made-up cursor, since it cannot move the real one. Whether the animation is smooth in the shell's transparent window on Windows, and whether a click beside the pill really lands on the window below, are steps 10 to 12 of the hand-test checklist in [windows-shell](../windows-shell.md).
